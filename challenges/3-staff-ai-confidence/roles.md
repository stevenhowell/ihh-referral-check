# Role cards

Drafted by the organizers from a planning conversation with IHH's leadership team and from public
descriptions of recuperative care. They are a starting point.

One fact shapes all three roles. In recuperative care, clients look after themselves: they take
their own medications and manage their own daily care. Staff monitor, teach, coordinate and
document. Staff do not give medications or make treatment decisions.

## Licensed vocational nurse (LVN) or medical assistant

**The shift.** Rounds on many clients. Vital signs. Watching clients take their own medications.
Checking blood sugars and dressings. Calling a clinic or home health when something is off.
Teaching: how to use a glucometer, when to take what.

**Where paperwork piles up.** Each contact needs a note in the electronic record, in a set format,
tied to the client's care plan goals. Notes get written at the end of the shift from memory or
from paper jottings, sometimes in more than one system. Roughly every 30 days the health plan
decides whether to keep paying for the stay, and those notes are the evidence.

**What worries them about AI.** Putting a client's information somewhere it should not go. A note
with their name on it that says something that did not happen. Whether this is the first step to
needing fewer staff.


## Referral coordinator

**The shift.** Hospital referrals arrive by fax and email through the day. Each is a packet of
chart pages to read against the admission criteria, with a two-hour target for an answer. Phone
calls to hospital case managers for whatever is missing.

**Where paperwork piles up.** Reading. Then writing the reply: yes, no with a brief reason, or a
list of what to send.

**What worries them about AI.** A tool that says yes to someone the site cannot safely care for, or
no to someone who needed the bed. Being blamed for a machine's mistake.


## Case manager

**The shift.** Helping clients with what comes after: housing assessments, benefits, identification
documents, appointments, transportation, and a plan for the day they leave.

**Where paperwork piles up.** Progress notes after every conversation. Care plans with goals and
dates. Letters and forms for other agencies. Looking up which resource fits which client.

**What worries them about AI.** Advice that sounds right and is out of date: a shelter that closed,
a phone number that changed, an eligibility rule that no longer applies.
