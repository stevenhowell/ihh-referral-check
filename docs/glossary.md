# Glossary

Plain-language meanings for terms you will meet in the criteria, the packets and conversations
with IHH staff.

## The setting

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Recuperative care** | A short stay in a safe place with a bed, meals and health monitoring, for someone who is homeless and too sick to recover on the street but does not need a hospital. Also called medical respite |
| **IHH** | Illumination Health + Home, the nonprofit partner for this event. Formerly Illumination Foundation; older documents say "IF" |
| **Referral** | A hospital's request that a patient be admitted to recuperative care |
| **Referral packet** | The referral form plus pages from the hospital chart, usually sent by fax |
| **Referral coordinator** | The IHH staff member who reads the packet and decides |
| **Case manager, social worker (CM, SW, MSW, LCSW)** | At the hospital: the person arranging a safe discharge. At IHH: the person helping a client with housing, benefits and next steps |
| **LVN** | Licensed vocational nurse |
| **Medi-Cal** | California's Medicaid program |
| **Managed care plan** | The health plan that runs Medi-Cal benefits in a county. In Orange County it is CalOptima Health |
| **CalAIM, Community Supports** | The Medi-Cal program under which health plans pay for services such as recuperative care |
| **Authorization (auth), LOA** | The health plan's approval to pay for the stay. LOA is a letter of agreement |
| **Home health** | Nurses or therapists who visit a person where they are staying, for wound care, IV medication or therapy |
| **SNF** | Skilled nursing facility. A higher level of care than recuperative care |
| **Coordinated entry** | The county's system for assessing people and matching them to shelter and housing |

## In the admission criteria

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **ADLs** | Activities of daily living: bathing, dressing, using the toilet, eating, moving around |
| **AOx4, AAOx4** | Alert and oriented to person, place, time and situation. Fully oriented |
| **DME** | Durable medical equipment: a wheelchair, walker, cane or shower bench |
| **Ambulate** | Walk |
| **Modified independence** | Does the task without another person's help, using a device or extra time |
| **Transfer** | Move from one surface to another, such as bed to wheelchair |
| **PT, OT** | Physical therapy and occupational therapy. Their notes say what a person can do physically |
| **Self-administer** | Take one's own medication without a staff member giving it |
| **Isolation, precautions** | Infection-control measures for a contagious condition |
| **MRSA** | A staph bacterium resistant to common antibiotics. Hospitals screen for it with a nose swab |
| **C. diff** | *Clostridioides difficile*, a contagious bowel infection that often follows antibiotics |
| **CIWA-Ar** | A score for alcohol withdrawal. Under 8 means little or no withdrawal |
| **Conservatorship** | A court arrangement in which someone else makes decisions for a person who cannot |
| **SI** | Suicidal ideation |
| **Command hallucinations** | Hearing a voice that tells the person to do something |
| **Hospice** | Comfort-focused care near the end of life |
| **DKA** | Diabetic ketoacidosis, a dangerous complication of diabetes |
| **A1C** | A blood test showing average blood sugar over about three months |
| **POC glucose** | A finger-stick blood sugar reading |
| **Sliding scale, correction scale** | An insulin dose that changes with the blood sugar reading |
| **EF, ejection fraction** | How well the heart pumps. Around 55 to 70 percent is typical |
| **Troponin** | A blood test for heart muscle injury |
| **EKG** | A tracing of the heart's electrical activity |
| **Sepsis** | A dangerous whole-body response to infection |
| **Cellulitis** | A skin infection |
| **Abscess, I&D** | A pocket of pus; incision and drainage is the procedure to open it |
| **Pressure injury, pressure ulcer** | A wound from lying or sitting in one position. Staged 1 to 4 by depth |
| **PICC** | A long IV line placed in the arm for weeks of IV medication |
| **Foley catheter** | A tube that drains the bladder |
| **COPD** | Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, a long-term lung condition |
| **O2 sat, SpO2** | Blood oxygen level. "Room air" means without extra oxygen |
| **BiPAP, CPAP** | Machines that support breathing through a mask |

## In the packets

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Face sheet** | The page with a patient's identifying, insurance and admission details |
| **H&P** | History and physical: the admitting doctor's summary of why the patient is there |
| **Progress note** | A doctor's daily update |
| **Consult** | A specialist's opinion |
| **MAR, medication list** | What the patient is taking |
| **Vitals** | Blood pressure, heart rate, breathing rate, temperature, oxygen level |
| **CBC, BMP** | Common blood test panels |
| **WBC** | White blood cell count. High often means infection |
| **Culture** | A lab test that grows bacteria to identify an infection |
| **PCR, antigen** | Two kinds of test for an infection, such as COVID |
| **MRN** | Medical record number |
| **DOB** | Date of birth |
| **NWB** | Non-weight-bearing: no weight on that leg |
| **PRN** | As needed |
| **s/p** | Status post: after. "s/p surgery" means after surgery |
| **ICD-10** | The coding system for diagnoses |

## Privacy

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **PHI** | Protected health information: health information that can be tied to a person |
| **HIPAA** | The U.S. law that protects PHI |
| **De-identified** | Stripped of everything that could identify the person. Harder to do well than it sounds |
| **De-identified packet** | A real record with every identifier blacked out. The Challenge 1 packets |
