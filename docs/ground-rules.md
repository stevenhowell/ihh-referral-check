# Ground rules

Illumination Health + Home (IHH) cares for people at one of the hardest moments of their lives.
These rules protect those people, IHH's staff and you.

## 1. No identifiable patient information

**No identifiable patient or client information. From anyone. For any reason.**

- The Challenge 1 packets are real referrals IHH shared for this event, with every name, date,
  facility, address, number, signature and barcode blacked out. **Do not copy them into your team's
  repository**, post them, or try to work out who anyone is. Point your code at them by path.
- Everything else is made up or already public: the clients in the scenarios are invented.
- Do not bring, type, paste, photograph or upload information about a real patient or client. That
  includes anything you know from your own job, even with the name removed.
- IHH staff will describe their work and answer questions. They will not share real records, and
  please do not ask them to.
- **If you see something that looks like real personal or health information, stop and tell an
  organizer straight away.** Do not copy it, screenshot it or put it into any tool.

## 2. Prototypes only

- Nothing you build today connects to, reads from or writes to IHH's systems.
- Nothing you build sends real messages to real people or services. Draft the message; do not
  send it.
- Nothing is installed at IHH today. If IHH wants to take a prototype further after the event,
  that is a separate conversation between IHH and the team.

## 3. Build in quality control

- AI makes mistakes. Build in a way to catch them. How is up to you.
- Nothing client-facing gives medical, legal or immigration advice.

## 4. Do not invent facts

- No made-up phone numbers, addresses, hours or eligibility rules for community services.
- No made-up page numbers or quotes from a referral packet.
- When the source does not say, your tool says it does not know.

## 5. Start today

- Your project starts at 9:30 AM. The template's own files do not count; what you add after
  9:30 does.
- Existing libraries, frameworks and public APIs are fine. The project itself is built today.
- No pre-work was expected, and none earns extra credit.

## 6. Open source, donated to IHH

- Your code lives in a public GitHub repository under an open-source license. MIT is the default.
- By submitting, your team agrees that IHH may use, change and build on what you made.
- Keep secrets out of the repo. API keys go in environment variables, not in code.
- Respect the terms of any data source or API you use.

## 7. Be straight in your demo

- Show what works. Say what is mocked.
- Say where your tool gets things wrong. Judges will trust you more for it.

## 8. Look after each other

This is a Claude Community event. The
[event terms](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12933905-claude-community-event-terms-and-acknowledgments)
you accepted at registration apply. Be kind, share the floor, and make room for teammates who do
not write code. Their knowledge of the problem is why this works.
